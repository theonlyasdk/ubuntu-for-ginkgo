M1
Audio & Sound Output ("Giving the Phone a Voice")
expand_more
In Plain Words: Video plays at 60fps, but internal speakers and headphones are silent. The audio hardware is now fully mapped: Qualcomm WCD9370/WCD9375 on SoundWire for 3.5mm headphones, Maxim MAX98927 amplifier on I2C-0 (0x3a) for the loudspeaker, and PipeWire Bluetooth A2DP for wireless earbuds.

settings_voice Hardware & Driver Topology

  • ADSP Remoteproc: Hexagon QDSP6 v66 @ 0xab00000 (34MB reserved RAM at 0x55300000).
  • Bolero Digital Codec: rx_macro (SoundWire 1) + tx_macro (SoundWire 2). WSA macro is disabled (no WSA881x).
  • Analog Headset: Qualcomm WCD9370/WCD9375 Aqstic attached to Bolero SoundWire slaves.
  • Wired Loudspeaker: Maxim MAX98927 Class-D amplifier on I2C-0 (0x3a, GPIO 27 reset) over Primary MI2S.
  • Wireless Audio: WCN3990 Bluetooth 5.0 via PipeWire A2DP. Works immediately today!
1

Step 1: Immediate Wireless Audio via Bluetooth A2DP

Because WCN3990 Bluetooth is active in ROM mode and crypto modules are loaded, WirePlumber automatically routes audio to paired wireless headphones.

bluetoothctl pair <HEADSET_MAC> && bluetoothctl connect <HEADSET_MAC> wpctl status pw-play /usr/share/sounds/alsa/Front_Center.wav

dmesg | grep -iE 'q6v5|apr|adsp|bolero|soundwire'

M2
Deep Sleep & Battery Longevity ("Pocket Survival")
expand_more
In Plain Words: Locking your phone should drop it into a deep sleep for all-day battery life. We previously kept the display powered because the Novatek touchscreen loses its volatile firmware (SRAM) when unpowered. Our recovery driver (ginkgo_touch_sync.ko) reloads touch firmware in <300ms on wake. Now we must formally validate end-to-end suspend/resume.

battery_charging_full Power & Sleep Subsystem Architecture

  • PM6125 Primary PMIC: System LDOs, PON power key, and hardware RTC (rtc@6000 via rtc-pm8xxx.ko).
  • PMI632 Secondary PMIC: Battery fuel gauge (qgauge@4800 via qcom-pmi632-qg.ko), display LCDB bias (±5.4V), and USB VBUS boost.
  • Volatile SRAM Recovery: ginkgo_touch_sync.ko streams novatek_ts_tianma_fw.bin over SPI SE2 upon wake.
  • Wakeup Sources: PM6125 RTC alarm IRQ (rtcwake), PM8941 PON Power Key, and Volume Up (pm6125_gpio5).
1

Step 1: Test Freeze (s2idle) Suspended State via RTC Timer

Use the PM6125 hardware RTC (rtc-pm8xxx.ko) to put the phone into s2idle for 10 seconds and automatically awaken it, verifying CPU rail sleep without crashing.

cat /sys/power/state # freeze mem cat /sys/power/mem_sleep # [s2idle] deep rtcwake -m freeze -s 10
2

Step 2: Validate Touchscreen SRAM Reload on Wake

Verify that /lib/systemd/system-sleep/touch-wake.sh triggers /sys/kernel/ginkgo_touch/resume upon wake and restores multi-touch tapping within 300ms.

echo 1 > /sys/kernel/ginkgo_touch/resume dmesg | grep -E "ginkgo_touch_sync|nvt" | tail -n 15
3

Step 3: Power Button & Volume Key Physical Wakeup

Test physical button wakeups (pon_pwrkey and PM6125 GPIO 5 gpio-keys) to verify instant display turn-on from sleep without requiring USB commands.

cat /sys/kernel/debug/wakeup_sources | grep -E 'pwrkey|rtc|gpio'
4

Step 4: Enable Display Timeout & Lockscreen in PowerDevil

Once suspend/resume is verified 100% stable, re-enable screen dimming (2 min) and blanking (5 min) in ~/.config/powerdevilrc to give the phone all-day battery endurance.

M3
Sensors & Screen Auto-Rotation ("Holding It Naturally")
expand_more
In Plain Words: When you rotate your phone sideways, it senses gravity using an accelerometer. Currently, the screen is fixed in portrait orientation (1080x2340). Schematics audit has located the exact chips, I2C buses, and interrupt GPIOs. Connecting them to Linux's Industrial I/O (IIO) subsystem gives KDE Plasma smooth automatic screen rotation and ambient light auto-brightness.

screen_rotation Schematic Pinout & Bus Mapping

  • Bosch BMI160 6-Axis IMU (U2303): On QUP1 SE0 (i2c@4c80000) at I2C address 0x68. ACCEL_INT on GPIO 80, GYRO_INT on GPIO 81.
  • Lite-On LTR-559ALS Ambient Light & Proximity (J2301): On QUP0 SE0 (i2c@4a80000) at I2C address 0x23. ALSP_INT_N on GPIO 91.
  • Asahi Kasei AK09918C 3-Axis Magnetometer (U2301): Shared on QUP1 SE0 at I2C address 0x0C.
  • Linux Subsystem: Kernel Industrial I/O (bmi160_i2c.ko, ltr501.ko) paired with userspace iio-sensor-proxy.service.
1

Step 1: Enable QUP0 & QUP1 I2C Buses in Device Tree

Enable qupv3_se0_i2c (GPIO 0/1) and qupv3_se5_i2c (GPIO 22/23) in sm6125-xiaomi-ginkgo.dts with BMI160 and LTR-559 target child nodes.

i2cdetect -y -r 0 # detects LTR-559 at 0x23 i2cdetect -y -r 1 # detects BMI160 at 0x68 and AK09918 at 0x0c
2

Step 2: Build & Stage IIO Kernel Modules

Build bmi160_i2c.ko, ltr501.ko, and ak8975.ko matching kernel #142 vermagic and place them in /lib/modules/ginkgo-extra/.

cat /sys/bus/iio/devices/iio:device*/name cat /sys/bus/iio/devices/iio:device0/in_accel_x_raw
3

Step 3: Connect iio-sensor-proxy to KDE Wayland

Start iio-sensor-proxy.service. It listens to IIO event channels, calculates orientation vectors, and broadcasts net.hadess.SensorProxy D-Bus signals to KWin for automatic Wayland screen rotation.

systemctl enable --now iio-sensor-proxy.service monitor-sensor # streams real-time orientation (normal, left-up, right-up)

M4
Cellular: 4G LTE, Calls & SMS ("Untethering from Wi-Fi")
expand_more
In Plain Words: The cellular modem is an independent Qualcomm Hexagon coprocessor running inside the Snapdragon 665. We already boot it up (that is how Wi-Fi initializes!), but we haven't linked it to the SIM card yet. Connecting the modem filesystem router (rmtfs) and starting ModemManager allows the phone to read your SIM, connect to cell towers (Airtel, Jio, Vi), send SMS, and route 4G mobile data (wwan0).

cell_tower Qualcomm Baseband & Modem Architecture

  • MPSS Remoteproc: Hexagon modem processor @ 0x06080000 in 126MB reserved memory (0x4b000000). Firmware: modem.mdt.
  • EFS NVRAM Storage: eMMC partitions (modemst1, modemst2, fsg, fsc) routed via rmtfs.service using carveout 0x89b01000.
  • IPC Router (QRTR): Qualcomm IPC socket family (AF_QIPC_ROUTER 42) bridging QMI messages over GLINK-SMEM.
  • IP Accelerator (IPAv4.2): On-SoC hardware DMA engine @ 0x05800000 (drivers/net/ipa) providing hardware packet routing for wwan0.
1

Step 1: Stabilize rmtfs EFS Bridge & QRTR Discovery

Verify rmtfs.service correctly bridges the modem's non-volatile NVRAM requests to eMMC partitions and confirm QMI service discovery with qrtr-lookup.

qrtr-lookup # shows services: 3 (NAS), 11 (UIM/SIM), 14 (WMS/SMS), 15 (WDS/Data)
2

Step 2: Unmask ModemManager & Probe SIM Card

Unmask ModemManager.service (ordered strictly After=rmtfs.service to avoid Hexagon watchdog crash loops) and interrogate the SIM card over QRTR.

systemctl unmask ModemManager.service && systemctl start ModemManager.service mmcli -L # lists detected modem: /org/freedesktop/ModemManager1/Modem/0 mmcli -m 0 --pin=1234 # unlocks SIM PIN
3

Step 3: Carrier Registration & Signal Verification

Enable the cellular radio transmitter and perform a network scan to register with local 4G LTE/GSM cell towers.

mmcli -m 0 -e # enables cellular radio mmcli -m 0 --3gpp-scan --timeout=120 qmicli -p -d qrtr://0 --nas-get-signal-info
4

Step 4: Mobile Data Packet Connection (wwan0)

Activate the QMI WDS data bearer through NetworkManager to instantiate the wwan0 interface and route Internet traffic over 4G LTE.

mmcli -m 0 --simple-connect="apn=your_carrier_apn" ip -br addr show dev wwan0

Non-Negotiable Project Directives for Agents & Developers

1. Keep mitigations=off: Spectre/Meltdown hardware mitigations are deliberately disabled in the kernel cmdline to maximize instruction retirement rate on Kryo 260 cores. Never re-enable them.
2. Out-of-Tree .ko Modules Only: Never flash a raw vanilla upstream v7.0 kernel over out/deployed-unpack/kernel (it lacks downstream DPU video pixel clock calibration and causes blank screens). Build extra drivers (e.g., bmi160_i2c.ko, wcd937x.ko, rtc-pm8xxx.ko) as out-of-tree modules matching #142 vermagic.
